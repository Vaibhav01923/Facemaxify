## What is facial harmony?

**Facial harmony** describes how well the features of a face work together as a whole. A harmonious face isn't necessarily one with any single perfect feature — it's one where the eyes, nose, lips, jaw and overall proportions are in balance with each other, so nothing stands out as too large, too small or out of place.

That's why two faces with similar individual features can look very different: one balanced, one slightly "off". Artists, orthodontists and cosmetic surgeons all talk about harmony for this reason — changing one feature in isolation can make a face less attractive if it no longer fits the rest.

This free **facial harmony score** combines seven proportions into one number out of 100, and shows how each one contributes, so you can see where your face is most balanced and where it isn't.

## How the facial harmony score is calculated

The tool runs Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh in your browser — your photo isn't uploaded — and places 478 landmarks on your face. It scores seven proportions, each out of 100:

| Metric | Weight | What's measured | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Symmetry | 25% | Balance of eight left–right feature pairs around your midline | Perfect balance |
| Jawline V-taper | 20% | Jaw width ÷ cheekbone width | About 0.78 |
| Midface proportion | 15% | Midface height (brows to nose base) ÷ lower face height (nose base to chin) | About 1.0 |
| Facial thirds | 15% | How evenly your face divides into upper, middle and lower thirds | 33% each |
| Nose harmony | 10% | Nose width ÷ face width | About 27.5% |
| Lip ratio | 7.5% | Upper lip height ÷ lower lip height | About 0.65 |
| Eye spacing | 7.5% | Gap between the eyes ÷ eye width | About 1.0 |

Each metric scores 100 at its target and loses points as you move away from it. The weighted average is your harmony score.

| Score | Result |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Exceptional harmony |
| 78–89 | High harmony |
| 64–77 | Good harmony |
| 50–63 | Moderate harmony |
| Below 50 | Developing harmony |

## Why harmony matters more than any single feature

Research on facial attractiveness keeps arriving at the same conclusion: balanced, typical proportions do better than extremes. In a classic 1990 study, Judith Langlois and Lori Roggman blended many faces into digital composites and found the composites were rated as more attractive than most of the individual faces — the averaging smoothed out disproportions and asymmetries. A 2010 study by Pallett, Link and Lee found that the most attractive eye–mouth and eye-spacing proportions were close to the average face.

In practice, that means a face with no standout features but excellent balance often looks more attractive than one with a striking feature that doesn't fit the rest. The harmony score is designed around that idea: no single metric dominates, and the score rewards being close to balanced targets across the whole face.

## How to read your harmony breakdown

Your total score is less useful than the pattern underneath it. Look for your lowest one or two metrics:

- **Low symmetry** is often a photo problem — retake the photo facing the camera directly. If it stays low, the [face symmetry test](/tools/face-symmetry) shows which zones are uneven.
- **Low jawline V-taper** means your jaw is either much wider or much narrower than your cheekbones. See the [jawline score](/tools/jawline-score).
- **Low midface proportion** means your midface is long or short compared with your lower face. The [midface ratio calculator](/tools/midface-ratio) explains what that means.
- **Low facial thirds** means one section of your face dominates vertically. Check the [facial thirds calculator](/tools/facial-thirds).
- **Low nose, lip or eye spacing scores** point to the smaller features. The [nose ratio](/tools/nose-ratio), [lip ratio](/tools/lip-ratio) and [eye spacing](/tools/eye-spacing) tools each go into more detail.

## Can you improve your facial harmony?

Some of it. Harmony is mostly about bone structure, but several things change how balanced your face looks:

- **Body fat** affects the jawline and midface more than any other factor.
- **Hairstyle** changes the visible proportions of your upper face — a fringe can shorten a long upper third.
- **Facial hair** can square off a soft jaw or balance a long lower face.
- **Brow grooming and makeup** can balance the eye area and the space between the eyes.
- **Cosmetic procedures** can change individual proportions, but because harmony is about the whole face, any change should be planned with a qualified specialist who looks at your face as a whole.

## Tips for an accurate harmony score

1. Face the camera straight on with your head level.
2. Keep a neutral expression with your lips relaxed and closed.
3. Take the photo from at least arm's length.
4. Use soft, even front lighting and pull back any hair covering your face.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is facial harmony?

Facial harmony is how well the features of a face fit together as a whole — the balance between the eyes, nose, lips, jaw and overall proportions. A harmonious face has no feature that looks out of proportion with the rest.

### How is the facial harmony score calculated?

It combines seven proportions — symmetry (25%), jawline V-taper (20%), midface proportion (15%), facial thirds (15%), nose width (10%), lip ratio (7.5%) and eye spacing (7.5%) — each scored by how close it is to a balanced target.

### What is a good facial harmony score?

On this tool, 64–77 is good harmony, 78–89 is high harmony and 90 or above is exceptional.

### Is facial harmony more important than symmetry?

Symmetry is part of harmony, but harmony is broader. A face can be fairly symmetrical but still unbalanced in its proportions, and small asymmetries are normal in very harmonious faces.

### Can facial harmony be improved?

Partly. Getting leaner, choosing a hairstyle and facial hair that balance your proportions, and grooming your brows all change how harmonious your face looks. Structural changes require cosmetic procedures, which should be planned with a qualified specialist.

### Is the facial harmony test free?

Yes. It's free, needs no signup, and analyses your photo in your browser.
