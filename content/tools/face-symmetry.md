## What is a facial symmetry test?

A **facial symmetry test** measures how closely the left and right halves of your face mirror each other. Every face is a little asymmetrical — one eye sits slightly higher, one side of the jaw is slightly wider, the nose leans a few degrees — and most of these differences are too small to notice in person. A symmetry test makes them measurable.

This free **face symmetry analyzer** works like a symmetrical face calculator: it measures eight pairs of features on your face, gives you a symmetry score out of 100, and shows a zone-by-zone breakdown so you can see which features are most and least balanced.

## How the face symmetry test works

When you upload a photo, the analyzer runs Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh in your browser — your photo isn't uploaded — and places 478 landmarks on your face. It then:

1. **Finds your facial midline** from three points on the centre of your face: the bridge of your nose, the tip of your nose and your chin.
2. **Measures eight paired features.** For each pair, it measures how far the left and right point sit from the midline.
3. **Compares the two sides.** Each pair gets a ratio — the shorter distance divided by the longer one. A perfectly balanced pair scores 1.0. If one side sits 10% further out, the ratio is about 0.91.
4. **Combines the pairs into a score.** The average ratio is mapped onto a 0–100 scale, where perfect symmetry is 100.

The eight zones it checks:

| Zone | Landmarks compared |
|---|---|
| Outer eye corners | Where your upper and lower lids meet at the outside of each eye |
| Inner eye corners | The corners of your eyes nearest your nose |
| Eye height | The highest point of each upper eyelid |
| Eyebrow arch | The peak of each brow |
| Mouth corners | The left and right corners of your lips |
| Cheekbones | The widest point of each cheekbone |
| Jaw angles | The corners of your jaw below each ear |
| Nostrils | The outer edge of each nostril |

Your results show each zone's deviation as a percentage, plus your most and least symmetrical features.

## What your symmetry score means

| Score | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | Highly symmetric | Exceptional balance across all eight zones |
| 78–89 | Mostly symmetric | Good balance with minor differences — a very common result |
| 65–77 | Moderate asymmetry | Measurable differences in a few zones; still well within normal |
| Below 65 | Notable asymmetry | Larger differences in some zones — check your photo angle first |

A low score is far more often caused by the photo than by your face. Even a slight head turn moves every feature on one side closer to the midline, which reads as asymmetry.

## Does facial symmetry make you more attractive?

Symmetry is one of the most studied factors in facial attractiveness. Research by Gillian Rhodes and others has found that people prefer more symmetrical versions of the same face, and evolutionary psychologists have suggested that symmetry signals healthy development.

But the effect is smaller than its reputation, for a few reasons:

- **Everyone is asymmetrical.** Small differences between the two sides are normal and part of what makes a face look natural.
- **Perfect symmetry can look odd.** When researchers build perfectly symmetrical faces by mirroring one half, the results often look artificial.
- **Other factors matter more in practice.** Averageness (proportions close to the typical face), skin quality, expression and grooming usually affect how attractive people find a face more than a few percent of asymmetry does.

Treat a symmetry score of 78 or above as good, and don't worry about chasing 100.

## Why your face looks different in photos and the mirror

If your face looks "wrong" in photos, asymmetry is usually the reason. You mostly see your face in a mirror — flipped left to right — so you're used to that version. A photo shows the unflipped face other people see, and any asymmetry appears on the "wrong" side. A 1977 study by Mita, Dermer and Knight found that people preferred mirror images of their own faces, while their friends preferred the true image. Neither version is more attractive; you're just more familiar with one.

## What causes facial asymmetry?

Most asymmetry is a normal part of how faces grow:

- **Genetics and development** — the two sides of the face develop slightly differently.
- **Teeth and jaw alignment** — bite problems or uneven jaw growth can shift the lower face.
- **Habits** — chewing mostly on one side may contribute to uneven jaw muscles, though evidence is limited.
- **Ageing and sun exposure** — the two sides of the face can age at slightly different rates.
- **Injury** to the nose or jaw.

**Important:** if part of your face suddenly droops or becomes asymmetrical — especially with slurred speech, arm weakness or confusion — call emergency services immediately, as it can be a sign of stroke. Sudden one-sided facial weakness can also be Bell's palsy, which also needs a doctor.

## Can you improve facial symmetry?

- **Photo technique** fixes most "asymmetry" seen in pictures: face the camera directly, keep your head level and avoid close-up selfies.
- **Grooming** can balance a face visually — your hair part, brow shaping and beard line all change how even a face looks.
- **Orthodontics and jaw surgery** can correct structural asymmetry caused by bite or jaw problems.
- **Cosmetic treatments** such as fillers can balance soft-tissue asymmetry. Discuss these with a qualified medical professional.

Claims that mewing, face yoga or sleeping position will noticeably fix adult facial asymmetry don't have good evidence behind them.

## Tips for an accurate symmetry score

1. **Face the camera directly** — your nose should point straight at the lens.
2. **Keep your head level**, not tilted to either side.
3. **Use a neutral expression.** Most smiles are slightly uneven.
4. **Use even light from the front.** Light from one side makes one half of your face look different from the other.
5. **Take the photo from arm's length or further**, using the rear camera if you can.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I test my facial symmetry?

Upload a straight-on, neutral-expression photo to this test. It finds your facial midline, measures eight pairs of features — eyes, brows, mouth corners, cheekbones, jaw angles and nostrils — and gives you a symmetry score out of 100 with a zone-by-zone breakdown.

### Is this a symmetrical face calculator?

Yes. It calculates how symmetrical your face is by comparing the distance of eight left–right feature pairs from your facial midline and combining them into a symmetry score.

### What is a good facial symmetry score?

A score of 78 or above is good and very common. 90 or above is exceptional. Scores between 65 and 77 show moderate asymmetry, which is still completely normal.

### Is anyone's face perfectly symmetrical?

No. Every human face has some asymmetry. Perfectly symmetrical faces made by mirroring one half of a face often look unnatural.

### Does facial symmetry affect attractiveness?

Somewhat. People tend to prefer more symmetrical versions of the same face, but the effect is modest, and features like averageness, skin quality and expression often matter more.

### Why does my face look asymmetrical in photos?

Because you usually see your face flipped in a mirror. Photos show the unflipped version, so any asymmetry appears reversed and looks unfamiliar. Head angle and close-up lenses also exaggerate asymmetry.

### Can you fix an asymmetrical face?

Photo technique and grooming fix most of what people notice. Structural asymmetry from bite or jaw problems can be treated with orthodontics or surgery, and soft-tissue asymmetry with cosmetic treatments. Mewing and face exercises don't have good evidence for fixing adult asymmetry.

Yes. It's free, needs no account, and analyses your photo in your browser.
