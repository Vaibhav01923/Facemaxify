## What is a PSL rating?

A **PSL rating** is a score out of 10 used in looksmaxxing communities to rate facial attractiveness. The name comes from three forums where the scale took shape — **P**UAhate, **S**luthate and **L**ookism — and it's still the standard shorthand on looksmaxxing sites today. (If you were looking for the pumpkin spice latte, you're in the wrong place, but you're welcome to rate your face while you're here.)

What makes the PSL scale different from an everyday "she's a 9" is that it's deliberately **harsh and anchored to the average**. On the PSL scale, 5 is meant to be the typical person, and each step above it represents a progressively rarer face. Ratings of 7 and up are uncommon, and 8+ is usually reserved for professional-model-level faces. A face that friends would casually call an 8 is often a 6 on the PSL scale.

This free PSL rating tool scores your face on that 1–10 scale using five measured features rather than opinion, and shows you a breakdown of each one.

## How this PSL test works

The tool uses Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh to place 478 landmarks on your face, entirely in your browser — your photo isn't uploaded anywhere. It then scores five features that looksmaxxing communities consider high-leverage, each on its own 1–10 scale:

| Feature | Weight | What's measured |
|---|---|---|
| Symmetry | 25% | How evenly seven left–right feature pairs sit around your facial midline |
| Jawline | 25% | Jaw width relative to cheekbone width, targeting about 0.78 |
| Canthal tilt | 20% | Whether the outer corners of your eyes sit higher or lower than the inner corners |
| Eye shape | 15% | How open your eyes are relative to their width — lower, more horizontal eyes score higher |
| Proportions | 15% | How evenly your face divides into upper, middle and lower thirds |

Your PSL score is the weighted average of those five, shown to one decimal place.

### Why these five features?

They map closely to what PSL communities talk about most:

- **Symmetry and proportions** are the foundation of facial harmony. Large imbalances are noticeable even to people who couldn't name them.
- **The jawline** is the most discussed feature in male looksmaxxing. A well-defined jaw that's slightly narrower than the cheekbones gives the face structure and a clean taper.
- **Canthal tilt and eye shape** together describe what forums call "hunter eyes": a slight upward tilt at the outer corners and a lower, more horizontal eye with little visible upper eyelid. A neutral tilt scores 5 on this component, and a positive tilt scores higher. You can check these in detail with the [canthal tilt calculator](/tools/canthal-tilt) and the [hunter eyes test](/tools/hunter-eyes).

Because the scale is anchored on 5 as average, a 6 is a genuinely good PSL score and a 7 is very good. If you'd like a gentler, more conventional rating of the same structure, the [face rating tool](/tools/face-rating) gives a 1–10 score that most people would recognise.

## A reality check on PSL culture

PSL ratings are popular because they're blunt, but it's worth being clear about what they are:

- **The PSL scale isn't scientific.** It grew out of forum culture, not research, and forum members often disagree by a point or more on the same face. This tool makes the scale consistent by tying it to measurements, but the scale itself is still a community convention.
- **Geometry isn't the whole picture.** Skin, hair, expression, body fat, style and confidence all change how attractive people find you in real life, and none of them appear in a landmark measurement.
- **Rating culture can get unhealthy.** If you notice yourself re-taking photos for hours, avoiding people because of your rating, or feeling distressed about a feature most people wouldn't notice, it's worth talking to someone you trust or a professional. Body dysmorphia is common and treatable.

Use the score as a structured way to understand your face — for example, to see whether your jawline or your eye area is your relative strength — not as a measure of your worth.

## How to get an accurate PSL rating

Small photo differences can move this score by half a point or more:

1. **Look straight at the camera** with your head level. A turned head lowers symmetry, and a tilted head distorts both canthal tilt and proportions.
2. **Use a relaxed, neutral expression.** Smiling or squinting changes eye openness and lifts the outer eye corners, which inflates the canthal tilt score.
3. **Don't take the photo too close.** Close-up selfies distort the centre of the face. Hold the phone at arm's length or further, or use a timer.
4. **Use soft, even light from the front** so the jaw and eye corners are clearly visible.
5. **Pull hair back** from your forehead and jaw so the model can place every landmark.

## Can you raise your PSL score?

Some components respond to change more than others:

- **Jawline** is the most improvable. Lower body fat makes the jaw and cheekbones read more clearly, and a beard can change the visible jaw shape.
- **Eye area** appearance can be affected by sleep, puffiness and brow grooming, though the bone structure behind canthal tilt doesn't change on its own.
- **Proportions and symmetry** are mostly structural. Better photos help; beyond that, changes require medical procedures, which should only ever be considered with a qualified specialist.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does PSL mean in looksmaxxing?

PSL stands for PUAhate, Sluthate and Lookism — three online forums where the rating scale became popular. In looksmaxxing, a PSL rating is a harsh 1–10 attractiveness score where 5 is average.

### What is an average PSL score?

On the PSL scale, 5 is meant to be average. That's why the scale feels harsh: a face most people would call good-looking often rates around 6, and 7 or more is uncommon.

### What is a good PSL score?

A 6 is a good PSL score and a 7 is very good. On this tool, 7.0–8.4 is labelled high tier and 8.5 or above is top tier.

### How is a PSL score different from a normal 1–10 rating?

Everyday 1–10 ratings are inflated — people use 7 or 8 for anyone attractive. The PSL scale is anchored so that 5 is the average face, which compresses everyone downward. A casual 8 is often around a PSL 6.

### Is the PSL scale scientific?

No. It's a community convention from looksmaxxing forums, not a research instrument. This tool makes it consistent by basing your rating on measured symmetry, jawline, canthal tilt, eye shape and facial proportions, but the scale itself isn't scientifically validated.

### Can I increase my PSL score?

Partly. Lowering body fat can sharpen the jawline, and better photos remove distortion that lowers several components. Bone structure — canthal tilt, proportions, most of the symmetry — doesn't change without medical procedures.

### Is there a PSL scale for women?

Yes, the same 1–10 scale is used for women, and this tool works the same way for everyone. Keep in mind that the jawline target is a general one, so very feminine, narrow jaws may score slightly lower on that component than their real-world appeal suggests.

### Why is my PSL rating lower than my friends rate me?

Partly because the PSL scale is harsher than normal ratings, and partly because friends react to your whole appearance — expression, style, personality — while this tool only measures facial geometry. Photo angle and distance can also lower the score.

### Is this PSL rater free and private?

Yes. It's free with no signup, and your photo is analysed in your browser rather than uploaded to a server.
