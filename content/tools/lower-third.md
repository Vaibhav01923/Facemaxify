## What is the lower third of the face?

The **lower third** of the face runs from the base of your nose to the bottom of your chin. It contains your philtrum, lips and chin, and its proportions have a big effect on how balanced, youthful and defined your face looks.

The classical guideline divides it in two at the point where your lips meet: the **upper lip takes up about one third**, and the **lower lip and chin together take up about two thirds**. When that balance is off — a long philtrum, a short chin, very thin or very full lips — the whole face can look different, even if your other proportions are ideal.

This free **lower third analyzer** splits your lower third into three parts — philtrum, lips and chin — and shows what percentage each one takes up.

## How the lower third analyzer works

The analyzer runs Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh in your browser (your photo isn't uploaded) and places landmarks at the base of your nose, the top of your upper lip, the bottom of your lower lip and the bottom of your chin. It divides your lower third into:

| Section | From | To | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Philtrum | Base of the nose | Top of the upper lip | About 22% |
| Lips | Top of the upper lip | Bottom of the lower lip | About 44% |
| Chin | Bottom of the lower lip | Bottom of the chin | About 34% |

It adds up how far each section is from its target and turns that into a score out of 100.

| Score | Result |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | Ideal lower third |
| 62–79 | Well-balanced |
| 45–61 | Slight imbalance |
| Below 45 | Notable imbalance |

The targets follow the same idea as the classical rule — a relatively short upper lip area above a longer lower lip and chin — but measure the lips as their own section, so you can see whether your philtrum, lips or chin is driving any imbalance.

## What your lower third breakdown means

- **A large philtrum percentage** means a long philtrum, which can make the upper lip look thinner and the face older. The [philtrum ratio calculator](/tools/philtrum-ratio) goes into more detail.
- **A small lip percentage** usually means thin lips; a large one means full lips. The [lip ratio analyzer](/tools/lip-ratio) compares your upper and lower lip.
- **A small chin percentage** often goes with a short or recessed chin, which can make the lower face look weak. A large one means a long chin. See the [chin ratio analyzer](/tools/chin-ratio).

## The lower third and ageing

The lower third changes noticeably with age. The upper lip lengthens and thins, the lips lose volume, and the skin along the jaw loosens. All of these push the philtrum percentage up and the lip percentage down — which is why a short philtrum and full lips are associated with youth.

## How to improve your lower third proportions

- **Facial hair** is the simplest way to change how the lower third looks — a moustache hides philtrum length and a beard can lengthen a short chin.
- **Lip filler** adds lip volume and can make a long philtrum look shorter.
- **Lip lift** surgery shortens a long philtrum permanently.
- **Chin filler, implants or genioplasty** can change chin length and projection.

Any medical option should be planned with a board-certified specialist looking at your whole lower face, because changing one part changes the balance of the others.

## Tips for an accurate result

1. **Keep your lips closed and relaxed** — no smiling or pouting.
2. **Keep your head level.** Tilting your chin up or down changes every vertical measurement.
3. **Shave or trim** if facial hair covers your upper lip or chin.
4. **Take the photo from arm's length or further.**
