## What is an AI face rating?

An **AI face rating** scores your face from a photo using measurements instead of opinions. Rather than asking strangers to "rate my face" — and getting answers that depend on their mood, taste and the photo you picked — this tool measures the geometry of your face and turns it into a consistent score.

This free **face rating tool** gives you a score out of 100 and a rating out of 10, built from five weighted measurements: symmetry, jawline, golden ratio proportions, facial thirds and nose proportion. You see each component, so you know what's lifting your rating and what's holding it back.

## How the face rating is calculated

The tool runs Google's MediaPipe Face Mesh in your browser — your photo isn't uploaded — and places 478 landmarks on your face. It then scores five features, each out of 100:

| Component | Weight | What's measured |
|---|---|---|
| Symmetry | 30% | How evenly eight left–right feature pairs sit around your facial midline |
| Jawline | 25% | Jaw width relative to cheekbone width, targeting about 0.78 |
| Golden ratio | 20% | Face length relative to face width, targeting the 1.618 golden proportion |
| Facial thirds | 15% | How evenly your face divides into upper, middle and lower thirds |
| Nose ratio | 10% | Nose width relative to face width, targeting about 27.5% |

Each component scores 100 at its target and loses points the further you are from it. The weighted total is your face rating out of 100; divide by ten for your rating out of 10.

**Why these weights?** Symmetry and the jawline carry the most weight because they have the clearest effect on how faces are rated — symmetry is the most studied single factor, and a defined jaw that's slightly narrower than the cheekbones gives the face structure. Proportion checks like the golden ratio and facial thirds capture overall balance, and nose width is a smaller but noticeable contributor to facial harmony.

## What your face rating means

| Score | Rating out of 10 | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 88–100 | 8.8–10 | Exceptional |
| 76–87 | 7.6–8.7 | Very attractive |
| 63–75 | 6.3–7.5 | Attractive |
| 50–62 | 5.0–6.2 | Average |
| Below 50 | under 5.0 | Below average |

This is a conventional 1–10 scale, similar to how people casually rate looks. If you want the harsher scale used in looksmaxxing communities, where 5 is the average face and 7 is rare, use the [PSL rating tool](/tools/psl-rating).

## AI face rating vs "rate my face" from people

Asking people to rate your face has real value — they react to your whole appearance, not just geometry. But individual ratings are noisy: they change with the photo, the lighting, the rater and even the faces they saw just before yours.

Research does show that when many people rate the same faces, they agree more than you might expect: a large 2000 meta-analysis led by Judith Langlois found strong agreement between raters within and across cultures. An AI face rating focuses on the measurable structure behind that agreement — symmetry, proportions and jawline — and gives you the same result every time for the same photo.

What it can't capture: skin quality, hair, expression, grooming, style and charisma. Those can move how attractive people find you by a lot, in either direction. Use your face rating as a structured look at your facial structure, alongside — not instead of — how you come across in person.

## Why your face rating might be lower than expected

Most surprisingly low ratings come from the photo rather than the face:

1. **A slight head turn** pulls features on one side towards the midline, lowering the symmetry score, which carries the most weight.
2. **Close-up selfies** distort the centre of the face, widening the nose and changing proportions. Take the photo from arm's length or further.
3. **Tilting your head** up or down changes facial thirds and the length-to-width ratio.
4. **Smiling** widens the lower face and changes jaw and mouth landmarks. Use a neutral expression.
5. **Hair covering your forehead or jaw** hides landmarks the tool needs.

Retake the photo straight on, in soft front lighting, with your hair pulled back, and compare.

## How to improve your face rating

- **Jawline** is the most changeable component — lower body fat makes the jaw and cheekbones read more clearly. The [jawline score tool](/tools/jawline-score) shows how your jaw measures up in detail.
- **Photo technique** improves symmetry and proportion scores immediately.
- **Grooming** — hair, brows, facial hair — doesn't change the geometry much but changes how balanced your face looks to people.
- **Structural features** like bone shape only change with medical procedures, which should only be considered with a board-certified specialist, never just to raise a score.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does the AI face rating work?

It places 478 landmarks on your face and scores five measurements — symmetry (30%), jawline (25%), golden ratio proportions (20%), facial thirds (15%) and nose proportion (10%) — then combines them into a score out of 100 and a rating out of 10.

### Can AI rate my face out of 10?

Yes. Divide your score by ten for your rating out of 10. A score of 72 is a 7.2/10, which this tool classes as attractive.

### What is a good face rating?

On this tool, 63 or above (6.3/10) is attractive, 76 or above is very attractive, and 88 or above is exceptional.

### Is the face rating accurate?

It's consistent — the same photo always gets the same score — and it measures real geometric features linked to attractiveness. It doesn't measure skin, expression, hair or style, so it describes your facial structure rather than how attractive you are overall.

### Why is my face rating different from what people say?

People rate your whole appearance in a specific moment, while this tool only measures facial geometry. Photo angle and distance also change the score. Retake the photo straight on from arm's length for the most accurate result.

### What's the difference between face rating and PSL rating?

Face rating uses a conventional 1–10 scale. PSL rating uses the harsher looksmaxxing scale, where 5 is the average face, and weighs canthal tilt and eye shape more heavily.

### Is the face rating free?

Yes. It's completely free, needs no signup, and your photo is analysed in your browser.
